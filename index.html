GENERAL ORDERS

Department Handbook

The governing standards of conduct, appearance, and duty performance for every sworn member of the Fort Worth Police Department roleplay division.

01Mission Statement

The Fort Worth Police Department exists to protect the life, property, and constitutional rights of every resident and visitor within our jurisdiction. Our mission is carried out through impartial enforcement of the law, proactive community engagement, and a standard of accountability that begins with every individual officer and extends through the full chain of command.

We measure our success not only by arrests made or citations issued, but by the trust the community places in us. Every member of this department, sworn or civilian, is expected to act as a steward of that trust in every interaction, on and off duty.

02Core Values

Integrity: Officers act consistently with honesty and the law, even when no one is watching. Fabrication of reports, evidence, or radio traffic is grounds for immediate suspension pending investigation.

Professionalism: Every interaction, from a routine traffic stop to a high-risk warrant, is conducted with composure and respect for the individuals involved, including suspects and offenders.

Service: The department exists for the community it serves. Officers are expected to treat calls for service, regardless of severity, with the same diligence and courtesy.

Accountability: Mistakes are corrected through supervision, retraining, and if necessary, discipline — not concealment. Supervisors are expected to document and address deficiencies promptly.

03Department Expectations

Officers are expected to report for shift briefing on time, in proper uniform, with assigned equipment inspected and serviceable. Radio checks are mandatory at the start of every shift before units are marked available.

All personnel are expected to remain reachable via department radio channels while on duty and to relay unit status (10-8, 10-6, 10-7, etc.) promptly and accurately so dispatch and command staff maintain an accurate picture of patrol coverage.

Members are expected to know and apply the contents of this handbook and the associated Standard Operating Procedures. Ignorance of policy is not a defense in an internal review.

04Professional Conduct

Officers will address members of the public, suspects, and fellow officers with respect and restraint. Use of profanity, racial or derogatory language, or demeaning remarks toward any individual is strictly prohibited and subject to immediate disciplinary review.

De-escalation is the preferred approach in all non-lethal encounters. Officers are expected to attempt verbal resolution before escalating to physical control tactics whenever the situation allows for it.

Off-duty conduct that reflects poorly on the department, including conduct within the broader roleplay community, may be subject to internal review at the discretion of Command Staff.

05Uniform Standards

The standard patrol uniform consists of the department-issue navy uniform shirt and trousers, duty belt with authorized equipment, agency patches on both shoulders, a visible name plate, and badge. Uniforms must be clean, pressed, and free of unauthorized modification.

Specialty units (SWAT, K9, Traffic, Motors) may be issued distinct uniform variants appropriate to their function, subject to approval from their division commander.

Personal grooming must meet department standards for a professional appearance. Visible unauthorized patches, pins, or modifications to the uniform are not permitted while on duty.

06Vehicle Standards

Marked patrol vehicles must be inspected at the start of each shift for damage, cleanliness, and functioning emergency equipment, including light bars, sirens, and dash camera systems. Any deficiency must be reported to a supervisor before the vehicle is placed into service.

Vehicles are to be operated in accordance with posted traffic laws except when responding to a Priority 1 call or engaged in an authorized pursuit, at which point department pursuit policy governs.

Unmarked and specialty unit vehicles must display authorized emergency equipment only, and their assignment is tracked through the division commander's roster.

07Use of Force

Force must be objectively reasonable, necessary, and proportional to the threat presented, consistent with the department's Use of Force Continuum (see SOP section). Officers will de-escalate whenever it is safe and feasible to do so.

Any application of force above compliant handcuffing must be documented in a use-of-force report and reviewed by a shift supervisor before the officer clears the scene.

Deadly force is authorized only when an officer reasonably believes it is necessary to prevent death or serious bodily harm to themselves or another person.

08Arrest Procedures

Officers must have probable cause prior to effecting a custodial arrest and should articulate that probable cause clearly in their incident report. Handcuffing will be double-locked and checked for fit once applied.

Miranda warnings must be read prior to any custodial interrogation. Statements made in response to un-Mirandized custodial questioning may be inadmissible and should be noted accordingly in the report.

Arrestees are to be searched incident to arrest, secured safely in the transport vehicle, and transported directly to booking absent an authorized detour for medical clearance.

09Traffic Stops

Officers will select a safe location to conduct a stop, activate emergency lighting, and notify dispatch of location, plate, and vehicle description prior to approach whenever possible.

Approach the vehicle from the driver's side rear quarter unless traffic conditions dictate a passenger-side approach. Maintain a visible position relative to vehicle occupants at all times.

Explain the reason for the stop, request documentation, and process the encounter (warning, citation, or arrest) as appropriate to the violation observed.

10Civilian Contacts

Consensual encounters, investigative detentions, and arrests are legally distinct and officers must be able to articulate which category applies to a given contact and the legal basis supporting it.

Officers will identify themselves by name and department at the start of an enforcement contact unless doing so would jeopardize officer safety or an ongoing investigation.

Members of the public are free to decline a consensual encounter and walk away absent reasonable suspicion of criminal activity justifying a detention.

11Pursuits

Vehicle pursuits will be initiated only for offenses serious enough to justify the risk to officers and the public, and must be continuously evaluated against that risk as the pursuit develops.

The primary unit will broadcast location, direction of travel, speed, and traffic conditions continuously. A supervisor may terminate any pursuit at their discretion.

Units not directly involved in the pursuit will not join without authorization from the supervising unit or dispatch, to avoid unnecessary congestion of the pursuit corridor.

12Report Writing

Reports must be factual, chronological, and free of subjective opinion beyond what is required to establish probable cause or officer observations. Use the reporting party's own words where relevant, in quotation marks.

All reports involving arrest, use of force, or property seizure must be submitted and approved by a supervisor before the end of the reporting officer's shift, absent an approved extension.

Body camera footage, when applicable, should be referenced by timestamp in the narrative to assist future review.

13Evidence Handling

All evidence must be logged, photographed in place prior to collection when feasible, and packaged to prevent contamination or loss. Chain of custody documentation begins at the moment of seizure.

Evidence is to be submitted to the evidence unit before end of shift. Any evidence retained overnight in a vehicle or locker must be logged with a supervisor's countersignature.

Officers who discover a break in chain of custody must report it to a supervisor immediately rather than attempting to correct documentation retroactively.

14Court Procedures

Officers subpoenaed for court appearances are required to attend in proper uniform or approved civilian attire and to review their report and any relevant footage beforehand.

Testimony must be limited to firsthand knowledge and documented facts. Officers should never speculate or embellish testimony to bolster a case.

Any scheduling conflict with a court appearance must be raised with a supervisor as early as possible so it can be resolved through the department's liaison with the court.

15Officer Safety

Officers are expected to maintain situational awareness at all times, request backup for any contact that presents an elevated risk, and avoid unnecessarily isolating themselves from support during high-risk contacts.

Cover officers should position themselves to observe the contact officer and any subjects without narrowing their own field of view or creating a crossfire risk with other units.

Any officer who reports feeling unsafe on a call has full authority to request additional units or disengage until backup arrives, without requiring further justification in the moment.

16Probationary Officers

Newly appointed officers serve a probationary period during which they are paired with a Field Training Officer and evaluated on report writing, radio discipline, tactical decision-making, and professionalism.

Probationary officers may not operate as a solo unit or serve as the primary unit on high-risk calls until cleared by their Field Training Officer and approved by a Watch Commander.

Performance concerns during probation are documented in weekly evaluations and shared directly with the officer to allow for correction before the probationary period concludes.

17Leave Policy

Requests for scheduled leave should be submitted to a shift supervisor with as much advance notice as reasonably possible so shift coverage can be arranged.

Extended leave of absence requests are reviewed by Command Staff and communicated back to the requesting officer with an expected return timeline where applicable.

Officers returning from extended leave may be required to complete a brief refresher review with their supervisor before resuming solo patrol duties.

18Attendance

Officers are expected to attend their assigned shifts and briefings punctually. Repeated unexcused absence or tardiness is addressed first through supervisor counseling and, if uncorrected, through formal discipline.

Officers unable to attend a scheduled shift are expected to notify their supervisor as far in advance as possible so shift coverage can be adjusted.

19Promotions

Promotions are based on demonstrated proficiency, time in grade, leadership potential, and a clean disciplinary record. Command Staff review candidates against a standard set of criteria rather than seniority alone.

Officers seeking promotion may be asked to complete a written or practical evaluation appropriate to the rank sought, in addition to a review of their service record.

20Discipline

Discipline follows a progressive model where appropriate: verbal counseling, written reprimand, suspension, and termination, scaled to the severity and frequency of the infraction.

Serious violations, including dishonesty, excessive force, or abuse of authority, may bypass progressive discipline and result in immediate suspension pending an internal affairs review.

Officers under review retain the right to explain their actions to the reviewing supervisor before final disciplinary action is determined.

21Resignations

Officers who wish to resign are asked to provide written notice to their supervisor and to complete an out-processing checklist covering the return of issued equipment, credentials, and vehicle access.

Exit interviews are optional but encouraged, and provide Command Staff with feedback used to improve training and department operations.

TACTICAL REFERENCE

Standard Operating Procedures

Numbered field procedures for common and high-risk scenarios. SOPs supplement, and do not replace, the judgment of the officer on scene.

S1Traffic Stops
  1. Select a safe stop location and activate emergency lighting.
  2. Notify dispatch of plate, location, and number of occupants.
  3. Approach with awareness of traffic flow and occupant movement.
  4. Request license and registration; explain the reason for the stop.
  5. Run the license and plate through dispatch before returning to the vehicle.
  6. Issue a warning, citation, or proceed to arrest procedures as warranted.
  7. Clear the stop and advise dispatch of the outcome.
S2Felony Stops
  1. Position patrol vehicle to provide cover, offset from the suspect vehicle.
  2. Request backup and wait for at least one additional unit before proceeding when feasible.
  3. Give loud, clear verbal commands from a position of cover.
  4. Direct occupants out one at a time, hands visible, walking backward toward officers.
  5. Handcuff and search each subject before moving to the next.
  6. Clear the vehicle once all occupants are secured.
S3Foot Pursuits
  1. Broadcast direction and description of the fleeing subject immediately.
  2. Continuously update location as the pursuit progresses.
  3. Evaluate terrain, visibility, and backup availability throughout.
  4. Disengage if the subject enters an unsecured structure without backup present, absent an immediate threat to others.
  5. Coordinate a perimeter with responding units if the subject is lost from sight.
S4Vehicle Pursuits
  1. Confirm justification for pursuit against the severity of the underlying offense.
  2. Broadcast location, speed, and direction of travel continuously.
  3. Designate a primary and secondary unit; all other units remain clear of the pursuit path.
  4. Request supervisor authorization to continue at defined intervals or upon significant risk change.
  5. Terminate immediately upon supervisor order or when risk to the public outweighs the need for apprehension.
S5Domestic Violence
  1. Separate involved parties upon arrival and interview individually.
  2. Identify the primary aggressor based on injuries, statements, and prior history.
  3. Document visible injuries with photographs where possible.
  4. Advise victims of available resources and protective order procedures.
  5. Effect arrest where probable cause for an offense exists, consistent with department policy.
S6Shots Fired
  1. Broadcast "shots fired" along with location and direction if known.
  2. Move to cover and assess for injured officers or civilians.
  3. Establish a perimeter and request additional units.
  4. Render first aid or request EMS for any injured persons once the scene is reasonably secure.
  5. Preserve the scene for evidence collection once secured.
S7Barricaded Subjects
  1. Establish an inner and outer perimeter at a safe distance.
  2. Notify a supervisor and request SWAT and negotiator response.
  3. Evacuate nearby civilians where it can be done safely.
  4. Attempt to establish communication only under the direction of a negotiator or supervisor.
  5. Hold position and maintain the perimeter until relieved or the incident is resolved.
S8Officer Down
  1. Broadcast "officer down" with exact location immediately.
  2. All available units respond code 3 unless directed otherwise by dispatch or a supervisor.
  3. Render immediate first aid and request EMS to stage nearby.
  4. Secure the immediate area to allow safe medical access.
  5. Designate a unit to escort or guide EMS to the officer's location.
S9Medical Calls
  1. Request EMS response with a description of the nature of the medical emergency.
  2. Provide basic first aid within the officer's training until EMS arrives.
  3. Secure the scene for EMS access, including crowd or traffic control as needed.
  4. Gather basic information for the incident report once the patient is stable or transported.
S10Search Warrants
  1. Confirm the warrant is signed, current, and matches the location to be searched.
  2. Brief all participating units on entry plan, roles, and known risks beforehand.
  3. Announce presence and authority prior to entry, absent a court-approved no-knock exception.
  4. Clear and secure the location before the search team begins collecting evidence.
  5. Log all seized items on scene with a corresponding inventory left with occupants.
S11Arrest Warrants
  1. Verify warrant validity and subject identity through dispatch prior to service.
  2. Conduct surveillance or briefing on the subject's last known location where practical.
  3. Approach with sufficient units to safely take the subject into custody.
  4. Advise the subject of the warrant and transport to booking upon arrest.
S12Evidence Collection
  1. Photograph evidence in its original position before collection.
  2. Package each item separately to prevent cross-contamination.
  3. Label each item with case number, date, time, and collecting officer.
  4. Log chain of custody beginning at the point of collection.
  5. Submit evidence to the evidence unit before end of shift.
S13K9 Requests
  1. Request K9 through dispatch, providing a summary of the situation and location.
  2. Establish a perimeter and clear the deployment area of uninvolved persons.
  3. Give way to the K9 handler's direction for deployment and announcements.
  4. Document the deployment and outcome in the incident report.
S14Air Support
  1. Request air support through dispatch with the nature of the call and last known location.
  2. Provide ground units' positions to avoid confusion during aerial tracking.
  3. Coordinate containment points as directed by the air unit.
  4. Maintain radio discipline to keep the air-to-ground channel clear.
S15Crash Investigations
  1. Secure the scene and request EMS if injuries are reported.
  2. Direct traffic around the scene to prevent secondary collisions.
  3. Photograph vehicle positions, damage, and relevant road conditions.
  4. Interview involved parties and witnesses separately.
  5. Complete a crash report documenting findings and any citations issued.
S16Active Shooter
  1. First arriving units move directly toward the threat to stop the killing.
  2. Form a contact team of available officers rather than waiting for full staging.
  3. Bypass injured persons initially; follow-on units handle rescue and medical tasks.
  4. Communicate suspect description and location continuously as the team moves.
  5. Establish rescue task force coordination with EMS once the threat is neutralized or contained.
S17Hostage Incidents
  1. Contain the location and establish a perimeter without direct confrontation.
  2. Notify Command Staff and request negotiator and SWAT response.
  3. Gather intelligence on hostage-taker demands, number of hostages, and layout.
  4. Defer all communication with the hostage-taker to trained negotiators.
  5. Maintain perimeter security until a resolution is reached.
S18Use of Force Continuum
  1. Officer presence and verbal commands — the baseline for nearly all contacts.
  2. Soft empty-hand control for non-compliant but non-threatening subjects.
  3. Hard empty-hand control or intermediate weapons for active resistance.
  4. Less-lethal tools for assaultive resistance that does not present a deadly threat.
  5. Deadly force, reserved solely for an imminent threat of death or serious bodily injury.
S19Miranda Rights
  1. Determine whether the subject is in custody and about to be interrogated.
  2. Read the standard Miranda warning clearly, in a language the subject understands.
  3. Confirm the subject understands each right before proceeding with questioning.
  4. Document the warning, time given, and the subject's response in the report.
S20Incident Documentation
  1. Note the time of dispatch, arrival, and clearance for every call.
  2. Record all involved parties, witnesses, and their statements.
  3. Attach photographs, body camera references, and evidence logs where applicable.
  4. Submit the completed report for supervisor review before end of shift.

GOVERNANCE

Department Policies

Core policy commitments that apply across every division and rank.

Professionalism

All personnel represent the department in every interaction. Courteous, composed conduct is expected regardless of how a situation escalates.

Bias-Free Policing

Enforcement decisions are based on behavior and evidence, never on race, gender, religion, or any other protected characteristic.

Body Camera Policy

Body cameras are activated for all enforcement contacts and calls for service, and footage is retained per department retention schedules.

Vehicle Operation

Patrol vehicles are operated safely and lawfully, with emergency equipment used only in accordance with pursuit and response policy.

Officer Appearance

Uniform and grooming standards maintain a consistent, professional department image across every shift and division.

Social Media Policy

Personal social media activity must not disclose confidential case information or misrepresent department positions.

Confidentiality

Case details, personnel records, and ongoing investigations are shared only on a need-to-know basis within the department.
